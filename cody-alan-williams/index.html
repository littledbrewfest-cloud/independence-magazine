Cody Alan Williams: Inside Robin Williams' Youngest Son's Life, Film Career, and Marriage

How Robin Williams' youngest child chose a behind-the-scenes film career, honored his father on his wedding day, and lives away from Hollywood noise.

By Sarah Jenkins
Chief Investigative Journalist & Senior Biographer
8 min read November 9, 2026

Growing up as the child of one of the most universally beloved comedic geniuses in modern cinematic history carries a unique set of circumstances. For Cody Alan Williams, the youngest son of the late Academy Award-winning actor and comedian Robin Williams, navigating that extraordinary family heritage never meant chasing the dazzle of red carpet flashbulbs. Instead, Cody deliberately chose a grounded, behind-the-scenes existence, mastering the technical crafts of motion picture production and establishing an independent identity defined by personal humility, dedicated craftsmanship, and quiet family devotion.

While millions around the globe celebrated Robin Williams for his electrifying comedic improvisations in Mrs. Doubtfire, Aladdin, and his poignant dramatic performances in Good Will Hunting and Dead Poets Society, at home in Northern California, Robin and his second wife, producer Marsha Garces Williams, prioritized providing their children with an upbringing anchored in normalcy, academic discipline, and emotional security.

Growing Up in San Francisco: Childhood in the Williams Household

Cody Alan Williams was born on November 25, 1991, in San Francisco, California. He arrived two years after his older sister, actress and filmmaker Zelda Williams, and eight years after his paternal half-brother, mental health advocate Zachary "Zak" Pym Williams, who was born to Robin's first wife, Valerie Velardi. Recognizing the intense pressures of the Southern California entertainment industry, Robin and Marsha made a conscious choice to raise their family in the San Francisco Bay Area, residing in the picturesque neighborhood of Sea Cliff and spending quiet retreats at their pastoral estate in the Mayacamas Mountains of Napa Valley.

This deliberate geographic separation from Hollywood allowed Cody and his siblings to experience a childhood characterized by neighborhood school routines, community sports, and private family dinners rather than paparazzi scrutiny. Marsha Garces Williams, who worked as a dedicated philanthropist and film producer on several of Robin's iconic projects, served as an unwavering anchor for the household, instilling strong values of respect, discretion, and intellectual curiosity in her children.

“My children don't care about the celebrity part of my work. At home, I'm just the guy who takes out the trash and tells bad dad jokes. What matters most is giving them the freedom to discover who they are on their own terms.”

— Robin Williams, San Francisco Chronicle Interview Archives (2001)

Those formative years shaped Cody's perspective on fame. While he accompanied his parents to occasional charitable galas—most notably benefits supporting the Christopher and Dana Reeve Foundation, honoring Robin's lifelong bond with actor Christopher Reeve—Cody developed an innate preference for listening and observing rather than performing center-stage.

Choosing Behind-the-Camera Film Production Over Acting

As Cody approached adulthood, he demonstrated a natural affinity for audio engineering, technical stagecraft, and the logistical intricacies of film production. Rather than enrolling in acting conservatories or seeking talent agency representation as a celebrity offspring, he pursued specialized education in sound production and cinematic equipment management.

He recognized early on that a film set functions through the collective efforts of hundreds of skilled tradespeople—grips, gaffers, camera operators, prop masters, and assistant directors—whose creative labor forms the bedrock of storytelling. By entering the industry as a crew member, Cody sought to earn his place strictly on the merits of his work ethic and technical reliability, deliberately avoiding the inevitable comparisons that accompany stepping in front of the lens.

Industry colleagues who worked alongside Cody on location frequently noted his unpretentious demeanor. He arrived early, hauled equipment cables without complaint, and rarely brought up his famous lineage, earning respect through punctuality, competence, and a collaborative spirit.

The Marvel Set Experiences: Hands-On Crew Work on Major Blockbusters

During the mid-2010s, Cody earned practical production credits on some of Hollywood's most complex cinematic undertakings. He joined the crew of Marvel Studios' ambitious superhero production Ant-Man (2015), directed by Peyton Reed and starring Paul Rudd. Working in second unit production logistics, Cody assisted with set operations, talent coordination, and practical equipment management during demanding shooting schedules in Atlanta and San Francisco.

His diligence on that production led to further opportunities, including crew assignments on Captain America: Civil War (2016), one of the highest-grossing ensemble films in box office history. Handling logistics on massive soundstages with extensive practical effects and complex stunt sequences gave Cody a comprehensive understanding of large-scale production pipelines. Colleagues respected his quiet focus, noting that he treated every crew member, from production assistants to department heads, with genuine kindness.

A Poignant Wedding Day: Marrying Maria Flores on Robin’s 68th Birthday

On July 21, 2019, Cody Alan Williams reached a momentous personal milestone when he married his longtime partner, Maria Flores. The couple had met six years earlier and weathered the devastating emotional storm of Robin Williams' passing in August 2014 together. When planning their nuptials, Cody and Maria made an intentional, deeply emotional decision regarding the date and venue.

They chose to exchange vows on July 21—the exact date that would have marked Robin Williams' 68th birthday. Furthermore, rather than renting an extravagant celebrity venue, they held the intimate ceremony at the San Francisco family home where Robin and Marsha had raised Cody and Zelda, and where Cody and Maria had first fallen in love years before.

“The 21st of July has meant many things to us over the years, but most recently, it had been a day of immense grief. Cody and Maria gave that day a new, radiant meaning—filling it with love, laughter, and yellow roses. Our dad was felt in every gentle breeze.”

— Zelda Williams, Public Remembrance Statement (July 2019)

The wedding ceremony was attended by immediate family, including mother Marsha Garces, sister Zelda, brother Zak, and close lifelong friends. Guests held yellow roses and lit memorial candles during a quiet moment of tribute, transforming an anniversary once heavy with grief into an enduring celebration of love, renewal, and generational resilience.

Safeguarding a Father's Memory: Estate Care and Modern Philanthropy

Following their father's death, Cody, Zelda, and Zak have worked with unified care to protect Robin Williams' intellectual, creative, and charitable legacy. The siblings have consistently emphasized mental health advocacy, supporting organizations that Robin passionately championed throughout his life, including the Challenged Athletes Foundation, St. Jude Children's Research Hospital, and military veterans' relief initiatives.

In recent years, the siblings have also actively collaborated to protect Robin's voice and likeness from unauthorized artificial intelligence exploitation, affirming their commitment to honoring the authentic human spirit that defined his life and artistry. While sister Zelda continues her creative trajectory as a film director and Zak serves as a leading public mental health speaker, Cody remains the quiet foundation of the family unit—thriving in his private marriage, continuing his passion for production craft, and carrying forward the profound lessons of empathy, laughter, and decency taught by his father.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Cody Alan Williams' profession?

Cody Alan Williams works behind the scenes in film and television production. He has served in crew and assistant director capacities on major Hollywood feature films, including Marvel Studios' Ant-Man (2015) and Captain America: Civil War (2016).

Who is Cody Alan Williams' wife?

Cody married his longtime partner Maria Flores on July 21, 2019. The wedding was held at the Williams family home in San Francisco on what would have been his late father Robin Williams' 68th birthday.

Who are Cody Williams' siblings?

Cody has an older sister, filmmaker Zelda Williams (born 1989), and an older paternal half-brother, mental health entrepreneur Zachary "Zak" Williams (born 1983).

Does Cody Alan Williams have public social media accounts?

No. Unlike many celebrity children, Cody maintains a strictly private lifestyle and does not operate public social media accounts, preferring to live outside the entertainment media spotlight.
