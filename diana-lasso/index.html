Diana Lasso: The Colombian Singer's Musical Roots, Marriage to Wayne Brady, and Life Journey

An investigative biography of Colombian vocalist Diana Lasso: her Latin music upbringing, early 1990s marriage to entertainer Wayne Brady, personal resilience, and musical advocacy today.

By Marcus Vance
Senior Investigative Biographer & Cultural Historian
9 min read October 26, 2026

In the expansive realm of Latin vocal music and early 1990s American entertainment, Diana Lasso holds a distinct place marked by creative passion and profound personal courage. Recognized in retrospective profiles as the first wife of multi-talented entertainer, television host, and Broadway performer Wayne Brady, Lasso’s life encompasses far more than a brief celebrity marriage. Born and raised in Colombia, Lasso is an accomplished vocalist who experienced the intense growing pains of entering the North American entertainment circuit at a young age, weathered deep battles with clinical depression, and ultimately rediscovered her voice through cultural advocacy and musical self-expression.

Similar to other artistic women who charted independent paths after early-career celebrity partnerships—such as Vanessa Villanueva moving beyond her marriage to Chris Perez or Kaynette Williams rebuilding her life in Oklahoma—Diana Lasso turned personal hardship into artistic purpose, embodying resilience and creative authenticity.

Colombian Childhood and Early Vocal Training

Diana Lasso was born in March 1975 in Bogotá, the cultural and musical capital of Colombia. Reared in a vibrant Latin American household where traditional cumbia, salsa, and romantic boleros filled the air, Lasso demonstrated exceptional vocal aptitude from early childhood. Possessing a rich, emotive contralto range with melodic flexibility, she sang in local choirs and school competitions, quickly gaining confidence as a performer.

Her family recognized her natural gift and supported her formal vocal training. Lasso studied Colombian folkloric music and contemporary pop styling, developing a deep appreciation for the storytelling power of Latin ballads. By her late teenage years, she set her sights on international opportunities, eager to bring her musical heritage to broader audiences in the United States.

Journey to the United States and Entering the Entertainment Circuit

In the early 1990s, Lasso moved to the United States, settling initially in Florida where vibrant Hispanic music circuits thrived. She performed at local cultural showcases, acoustic lounges, and community festivals. At the same time, a young Orlando performer named Wayne Brady was honing his skills in community musical theater and improvisational comedy.

The two met in 1993 through shared artistic circles in Florida. At that time, Brady had not yet achieved the nationwide fame of Whose Line Is It Anyway? or The Wayne Brady Show; he was an ambitious, hardworking 21-year-old actor working at theme parks and local clubs. Brady was immediately drawn to Lasso’s expressive voice, captivating beauty, and infectious warmth, and the two young performers quickly became inseparable.

Marriage to Wayne Brady (1993–1995): An Early Chapter

On New Year’s Eve, December 31, 1993, Diana Lasso and Wayne Brady married in an intimate ceremony. At the time, Lasso was just 18 years old and Brady was 21. While their marriage was rooted in genuine affection, the young couple soon faced severe real-world challenges that placed immense strain on their relationship.

Both were struggling financially, trying to book gigs while navigating the expensive realities of living in high-cost entertainment markets. Furthermore, the relentless travel required by Brady’s auditions and Lasso’s vocal bookings created physical distance. After approximately one year and nine months of marriage, on September 21, 1995, the couple officially finalized their divorce. In subsequent interviews years later, Brady spoke respectfully of Lasso, acknowledging that both were simply too young and unequipped for the emotional demands of marriage at that stage of their lives.

Personal Struggles, Mental Health Awareness, and Finding Strength

Following her divorce from Brady, Lasso faced a prolonged and deeply challenging period. Navigating life as a single immigrant artist, she experienced severe bouts of clinical depression and loneliness. Feeling disconnected from her roots, she returned to Colombia for a time to heal among family and close friends.

Rather than hiding her struggles, Lasso later spoke candidly about the critical importance of mental health care, seeking professional counseling, and relying on family faith. Her openness resonated deeply within Latin communities, where mental health topics have historically carried cultural stigma. Lasso’s journey toward emotional recovery became an inspiring testimony of personal perseverance.

Return to Music: Traditional Latin Ballads and Creative Revival

With renewed emotional strength, Diana Lasso reclaimed her musical identity. She released independent recordings, including emotive singles such as 'Esta Vez,' which showcased her vocal range and heartfelt songwriting. Her music blends contemporary pop ballads with acoustic Colombian guitar arrangements, celebrating themes of personal redemption, love, and cultural identity.

Lasso performed across Latin American cultural festivals, radio broadcasts, and intimate venues in both Colombia and the United States. Rather than pursuing commercial chart trends, she dedicated her music to listeners who appreciate emotional authenticity and poetic lyricism.

Life in Colombia and the United States Today

In 2026, Diana Lasso maintains a balanced lifestyle split between Colombia and Florida. She became a mother to a daughter whom she has raised with deep love, instilling in her the same cultural values and artistic appreciation that defined her own childhood.

Lasso is widely admired in regional music communities for her mentorship of aspiring young Colombian vocalists. She frequently participates in music education workshops and community fundraisers, using her platform to encourage women to pursue creative education and practice emotional wellness.

Professional Net Worth and Royalties in 2026

As of 2026, financial research analysts estimate Diana Lasso’s net worth at between $200,000 and $400,000 USD. This modest, stable financial profile is derived entirely from her independent recording catalog, performance fees, vocal coaching services, and local music publishing royalties.

Unlike sensational internet articles that occasionally confuse Diana Lasso with Brady’s second wife, Mandie Taketa (who co-parents Brady’s daughter Maile), Lasso has lived an independent, self-made life completely separate from Brady’s Hollywood wealth. She has never sought public alimony disputes or media exploitation, earning widespread respect for her personal dignity.

Reflections on Legacy and Quiet Living in 2026

At 51 years of age in 2026, Diana Lasso looks back on her life journey with gratitude and peace. While entertainment trivia will always note her as Wayne Brady’s first wife, Lasso’s enduring legacy is defined by her music, her resilience in overcoming depression, and her devotion to her family and Colombian culture.

Her story stands as an inspiring reminder that a youthful marriage does not define an artist’s destiny—it is merely one chapter in a lifelong song of self-discovery, faith, and artistic integrity.
