Jacqueline Ray: Inside Her Marriage to Tom Selleck, Her Age, Career, Son Kevin, and Life Today

An extensive biographical retrospective on former 1970s actress Jacqueline Ray: her decade-long marriage to Tom Selleck, screen credits, son Kevin Selleck, and historical timeline.

By Marcus Vance
Senior Cultural Affairs Editor
10 min read October 20, 2026

During the vibrant golden age of American network television in the 1970s and early 1980s, few Hollywood couples possessed as striking an aesthetic presence as Tom Selleck and his first wife, model and actress Jacqueline Ray. Long before Selleck became a global household name with his defining role in Magnum, P.I., Ray was already an established California fashion model and working screen performer. Together for eleven formative years between 1971 and 1982, the couple navigated the steep financial and emotional climb of Hollywood audition circuits, pilot rejections, and ultimate international stardom.

Decades after their respectful divorce, public curiosity regarding Jacqueline Ray remains extraordinarily high. Audiences and media historians frequently seek verified biographical facts concerning her exact age, her acting filmography alongside Selleck, her son Kevin Selleck’s life, and her later private timeline. Here is the comprehensive, fact-checked account of Jacqueline Ray’s life, artistic career, and lasting historical legacy.

An Overview of Jacqueline Ray’s Life and Public Profile

Jacqueline Ray is an American former actress and print model whose career flourished during the late 1960s, 1970s, and 1980s. Blessed with statuesque poise, classic features, and strong screen charisma, Ray worked across both feature films and popular television series. She gained widespread attention as the first wife of iconic actor Tom Selleck, standing by his side throughout the lean early years of his career prior to his breakout success in Hawaii.

Much like other celebrated figures in television history whose marriages occurred during critical career transitions—such as Juanita Vanoy during Michael Jordan's rise and Cathleen Oveson alongside Kenny Johnson—Jacqueline Ray’s partnership with Selleck represented an essential chapter in the personal narrative of one of Hollywood’s most enduring leading men.

Birth Date, California Roots, and Real Age

Jacqueline Mary Ray was born on July 17, 1952, in Burbank, California. While some legacy internet databases erroneously list her birth year as 1945, verified public vital statistics and archival union records identify July 1952 as her documented birth date. As of 2026, Jacqueline Ray is 74 years old.

Raised in Southern California during the postwar boom, Ray was drawn to photography and theatrical arts from an early age. By her late teens, her tall frame and striking features caught the attention of leading Los Angeles modeling agencies, quickly propelling her into commercial print spreads and national television commercials.

Early Modeling and Acting Career in Television and Film

Jacqueline Ray made her initial transition from commercial modeling to feature films with an uncredited role in the popular 1967 espionage comedy In Like Flint, starring James Coburn. The experience solidified her desire to pursue professional screen acting, leading to formal dramatic training in Los Angeles.

Throughout the 1970s, Ray worked steadily across multiple genres. In 1980, she took on a prominent role in the cult science-fiction film The Killings at Outpost Zeta, followed by an appearance in Beyond the Universe (1981). However, her most widely viewed television performances came via guest spots on CBS’s smash-hit detective series Magnum, P.I.. Ray appeared in the Season 1 episode "J. 'Digger' Doyle" (1981) as Ms. Roaches, and returned in the Season 3 episode "Birdman of Budapest" (1983) portraying the character Lisa. Her on-screen chemistry and effortless professionalism made her guest appearances memorable for loyal fans of the franchise.

The Eleven-Year Marriage to Tom Selleck (1971–1982)

The defining public relationship of Jacqueline Ray’s life began in the late 1960s when she met a young, struggling commercial actor and former college athlete named Tom Selleck. At the time, Selleck was landing minor guest roles on shows like Lancer and appearing in Safeguard soap commercials, far from guaranteed stardom. Ray was already enjoying steady work as a model and raising her young son, Kevin.

The couple fell deeply in love and married on May 15, 1971. Shortly after their wedding, Selleck took the formal, loving step of legally adopting Jacqueline's five-year-old son Kevin, giving the boy his surname and treating him as his own child from that day forward. For over a decade, Selleck, Ray, and Kevin lived as a close-knit family unit in Southern California.

During the 1970s, Ray provided crucial emotional stability as Selleck filmed numerous failed television pilots. However, when Selleck finally landed the title role of Thomas Magnum in 1980, the demanding filming schedule required him to relocate to Oahu, Hawaii, for ten months out of every year. The physical distance, coupled with the overwhelming onslaught of global fame, placed immense strain on their union. After eleven years of marriage, Jacqueline Ray and Tom Selleck mutually filed for divorce, which was finalized on August 10, 1982. Selleck has always spoken of Ray with dignity and high respect, noting that she stood by him through the hardest financial years of his youth.

Her Son Kevin Selleck and the Tom Selleck Fatherhood Bond

One of the most enduring positive outcomes of Jacqueline Ray's marriage to Tom Selleck was the lifelong bond established between Selleck and her son, Kevin Selleck (born Kevin S. Poole in 1966). Even following the 1982 divorce, Tom Selleck never wavered in his commitment as Kevin’s legal and emotional father.

Kevin grew up with Tom's active mentorship, attending high school and college in California before pursuing music as the drummer for the 1990s rock band Tonic, whose debut album Lemon Parade achieved multi-platinum success with the hit single "If You Could Only See." Tom Selleck consistently supported Kevin through both his musical milestones and personal health challenges, demonstrating an admirable standard of parental fidelity.

Later Life, Legal Inquiries, and Private Circumstances

Following her divorce from Selleck, Jacqueline Ray gradually withdrew from public Hollywood life. In March 1992, she married Clarence Witmer, though that union eventually dissolved quietly. Ray chose not to participate in celebrity reality television or publish tell-all memoirs, maintaining strict privacy regarding her personal affairs.

In later years, Ray faced significant family hardships. In 2014, she was involved in a deeply troubling California legal case stemming from family conflicts involving her daughter Umeko and son-in-law, which resulted in a custodial sentence. Throughout this painful ordeal, those close to the family noted that Ray's motives had been driven by deep distress over her daughter's well-being. Following the resolution of legal proceedings, Ray returned to complete seclusion away from media cameras, focusing entirely on personal reconciliation and private family recovery.

Jacqueline Ray’s Estimated Net Worth and Assets

As of 2026, Jacqueline Ray’s estimated net worth stands at approximately $2 Million. Her financial portfolio is derived from past entertainment royalties, SAG-AFTRA pension benefits, divorce settlements from the early 1980s, and long-term Southern California real estate investments held across past decades.

While Tom Selleck’s personal net worth has grown to over $45 Million due to his historic ten-season run on Magnum, P.I. and his fourteen seasons leading CBS’s Blue Bloods, Ray has lived modestly, valuing quiet seclusion and avoiding any financial exploitation of her famous ex-husband's name.

Where Is Jacqueline Ray Now?

Today, at 74 years of age, Jacqueline Ray lives a quiet, retired life in California. She maintains no public social media accounts and firmly declines television interview invitations. Friends and former colleagues remember her as a gracious, exceptionally beautiful woman who played an indispensable role during the formative years of one of television's greatest stars.
