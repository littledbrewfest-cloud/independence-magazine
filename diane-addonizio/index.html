Who Is Diane Addonizio? Inside the Life and Career of Howie Long's Wife of Over 40 Years

From Villanova University and USC Gould Law School to raising NFL champions Chris and Kyle Long and philanthropic leadership.

Investigative Features & Senior Editor

In the storied history of American sports dynasties, the Long family occupies a rarefied position. Patriarch Howie Long is an NFL Hall of Fame defensive end and beloved television analyst; eldest son Chris Long won back-to-back Super Bowls and was named Walter Payton NFL Man of the Year; middle son Kyle Long earned three Pro Bowl honors; and youngest son Howie Long Jr. serves as a key front-office personnel scout. Yet behind this athletic dominance stands the intellectual anchor and moral compass of the family: Diane Addonizio.

An accomplished corporate attorney educated at Villanova University and the USC Gould School of Law, Addonizio has spent more than forty-two years steering her household with discipline, grace, and an unwavering commitment to education and philanthropic service. Often called the "First Lady of Football," she has established herself as a leading advocate for youth athletic safety and community empowerment.

Early Life, Education, and Legal Training

Diane Addonizio was born in 1962 in Red Bank, New Jersey, into an Italian-American Catholic household where academic discipline and personal responsibility were paramount. From early childhood, she demonstrated exceptional scholastic abilities, participating in debate societies and civic leadership programs.

She enrolled at Villanova University outside Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, graduating in 1982 with a Bachelor of Arts in Political Science. Her sharp analytical mind led her to pursue legal studies at the prestigious USC Gould School of Law in Los Angeles, California, where she earned her Juris Doctor (J.D.) degree. Following her admission to the bar, she practiced corporate law in Southern California, advising clients on contract disputes and corporate compliance.

Collegiate Courtship and Meeting Howie Long at Villanova

Diane first crossed paths with Howie Long in the autumn of 1978 during their freshman year at Villanova University. At the time, Long was a towering, intense defensive lineman from Charlestown, Massachusetts, coping with a turbulent childhood and establishing his identity on the football field. Addonizio was an academically focused undergraduate who had little interest in the machinations of collegiate athletics.

Their first date has since become a piece of sports folklore: Howie invited Diane to his dorm room to watch game film of opposing offensive lines. While not the standard romantic evening, Addonizio was struck by his dedication, honesty, and vulnerability beneath his rugged exterior. The couple formed an inseparable partnership that grounded Long through his collegiate accolades and his selection in the second round of the 1981 NFL Draft by Al Davis and the Oakland Raiders.

Forty-Two Years of Marriage: June 27, 1982

On June 27, 1982, Diane Addonizio and Howie Long married in a traditional Catholic ceremony in New Jersey, shortly after Long's rookie season with the Raiders. Their marriage coincided with the franchise's move to Los Angeles, placing the young couple in the center of Southern California's sports and entertainment spotlight.

Raising an NFL Dynasty: Chris, Kyle, and Howie Jr.

The couple welcomed three sons: Christopher Howard Long in 1985, Kyle Howard Long in 1988, and Howard Long Jr. in 1990. Despite their father's immense fame, Diane instituted strict household rules prioritizing educational performance, humility, and respect above athletic achievement.

Her deliberate approach to raising children away from media sensationalism shares common ground with Wanda Hutchins, who prioritized educational discipline and emotional security for her children with Michael Strahan. The achievements of the three Long sons reflect their mother's balanced guidance:

  • Chris Long: After starring at the University of Virginia, Chris was selected No. 2 overall in the 2008 NFL Draft. He played eleven seasons, winning back-to-back Super Bowls with the New England Patriots (Super Bowl LI) and Philadelphia Eagles (Super Bowl LII). In 2018, he received the prestigious Walter Payton NFL Man of the Year Award after donating his entire season salary to educational equity programs.
  • Kyle Long: A formidable offensive lineman at the University of Oregon, Kyle was drafted in the first round by the Chicago Bears in 2013, earning three consecutive Pro Bowl selections (2013–2015) and cementing his status as one of the league's premiere guards.
  • Howie Long Jr.: Followed his family into football, excelling at St. Anne's-Belfield and Virginia before transitioning into NFL player personnel and scouting for the Las Vegas Raiders.

Advocacy for Youth Sports Safety and Parental Education

Witnessing the immense physical toll of football on her husband and sons firsthand, Diane Addonizio became a prominent voice in sports medicine and parental safety education. Rather than romanticizing athletic violence, she openly discussed the anxieties mothers face regarding concussions, orthopedic trauma, and long-term cognitive health.

She contributed to educational panels with USA Football and co-authored practical guides for youth athletic parents, advocating for baseline cognitive testing, improved helmet technology, and certified athletic trainers at all high-school sporting events. Her outspoken yet constructive approach earned praise from pediatric neurologists and athletic directors nationwide.

Philanthropic Leadership and The Chris Long Foundation

Beyond her legal acumen, Addonizio has served as a driving force behind the Long family's extensive philanthropic endeavors. She actively supports The Chris Long Foundation and its flagship international initiative, Waterboys, which unites professional athletes and fans to fund clean, sustainable water wells for rural communities in East Africa.

To date, the Waterboys initiative has provided clean drinking water to over 500,000 people across Tanzania and Kenya, alongside domestic clean water projects addressing infrastructure deficits in underserved communities in Virginia and across the American South.

Life Today in Charlottesville and Estimated Net Worth

Today, Diane and Howie Long reside on a scenic private estate in Ivy, Virginia, near Charlottesville, where they raised their children. They enjoy a peaceful country lifestyle surrounded by their grandchildren, horses, and close collegiate friends.

The Long family household maintains an estimated net worth between $20 Million and $25 Million, reflecting Howie Long's NFL playing career, his three-decade tenure as the premier studio analyst on Fox NFL Sunday, Diane's legal investments, and significant agricultural and commercial real estate holdings in Virginia and California.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Diane Addonizio and Howie Long have been married for over 42 years. They tied the knot on June 27, 1982, after meeting as freshmen at Villanova University in 1978.

What is Diane Addonizio's professional and educational background?

She is a trained attorney. She earned her Bachelor of Arts in Political Science from Villanova University in 1982 and subsequently received her Juris Doctor (J.D.) from the USC Gould School of Law in Los Angeles, later practicing corporate law.

Who are Diane Addonizio and Howie Long's children?

They have three sons: two-time Super Bowl champion Chris Long (b. 1985), three-time Pro Bowl offensive guard Kyle Long (b. 1988), and NFL executive scout Howie Long Jr. (b. 1990).

What philanthropic causes does Diane Addonizio support?

She is an active trustee for The Chris Long Foundation and the Waterboys clean water initiative in East Africa, and serves as an advocate for youth concussion safety and parental education in youth sports.

Where do Diane and Howie Long live today?

They reside in Ivy, Virginia, near Charlottesville, maintaining a private country estate close to the University of Virginia where their sons attended college.
