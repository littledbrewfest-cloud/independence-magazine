Who Is Logan Romero Plant? The Biography of Robert Plant's Son, Music, and Beavertown Brewery

Stepping out from Led Zeppelin's shadow to front rock bands and build the £100+ million craft beer powerhouse Beavertown Brewery.

By Marcus Vance
Investigative Features & Senior Editor
8 min read October 12, 2026

Being the son of Robert Plant—the legendary golden-curled voice of Led Zeppelin and one of the most revered rock frontmen in human history—presents an almost insurmountable artistic shadow. Many children of rock royalty either retreat completely into anonymity or become perpetual custodians of their parents' nostalgia. Logan Romero Plant took a radically different path: after years of fronting independent rock outfits and touring the world in packed vans, he poured his creative energy into the culinary arts, establishing Beavertown Brewery and transforming it into a £100+ million commercial empire that redefined the modern British craft beer revolution.

From experimenting with a secondhand 50-liter rice cooker in the kitchen of a North London barbecue restaurant to opening the monumental "Beaverworld" production facility in Enfield and ultimately orchestrating a historic acquisition by Heineken, Logan Plant proved that visionary grit, authentic branding, and uncompromising product quality can forge an iconic legacy entirely his own.

Rock Royalty Heritage and English Countryside Upbringing

Logan Romero Plant was born on January 21, 1979, in the West Midlands region of England. He was the third child born to Led Zeppelin frontman Robert Plant and his Anglo-Indian wife Maureen Wilson. His older sister Carmen was born in 1968, and an older brother, Karac, was born in 1972.

Logan's early years were overshadowed by a profound family tragedy. In July 1977, while Led Zeppelin was touring the United States, five-year-old Karac passed away suddenly from a severe stomach infection. The loss devastated Robert and Maureen, prompting Plant to step away from music temporarily and return to his family estate in Worcestershire. Logan's birth eighteen months later brought renewed light and healing to the Plant household.

Despite his father's unimaginable fame, Robert and Maureen went to great lengths to provide their children with a grounded upbringing. Logan grew up immersed in the pastoral beauty of rural Shropshire and Worcestershire, playing football, exploring the Welsh marches, and attending local state schools away from metropolitan media circuits.

Athletic Pursuits and Musical Exploration

During his adolescence, Logan initially harbored ambitions in professional athletics. A gifted and disciplined athlete, he excelled as a semi-professional football player, training in the youth developmental ranks of clubs in the English Midlands. However, the genetic pull of songwriting and live performance eventually asserted itself.

In his early twenties, Logan formed the blues-rock band Black Country Band, followed by the critically acclaimed alternative rock group Sons of Albion. Serving as lead vocalist and primary songwriter, Logan deliberately refused to trade on his father's surname or solicit major-label favors. Sons of Albion embarked on grueling tours across the UK, Europe, and the United States, performing at legendary indie venues and festival circuits.

The American Road Trip: Barbecue and the Craft Beer Epiphany

In the late 2000s, while touring the American South and West Coast with Sons of Albion, Logan experienced an epiphany that would permanently alter his life. Visiting craft breweries in San Diego, Colorado, and Portland, and sampling authentic low-and-slow barbecue in Texas, he realized that the British beverage market was decades behind American craft innovation.

In the UK, the beer landscape was still largely dominated by bland industrial lagers and traditional room-temperature cask ales. Logan fell in love with intensely hopped American IPAs, vibrant citrus notes, and the communal taproom culture. Returning to London, he made the daring decision to hang up his touring microphone and immerse himself in commercial brewing science.

Founding Beavertown Brewery in 2011: The Rice Cooker Era

In 2011, Logan Plant partnered with chef and restaurateur Byron Knight to open Duke's Brew & Que, an American barbecue smokehouse in Haggerston, East London. In the cramped kitchen, Logan installed a crude 50-liter brewing kit that he converted from an industrial rice cooker and stainless-steel jam pots.

Working late into the night after restaurant service, Plant experimented tirelessly with hop varieties from the Pacific Northwest, dry hopping techniques, and water minerality. He christened the operation Beavertown Brewery—a nod to the historic Cockney nickname for the historic De Beauvoir Town district of Hackney where he lived and worked.

Neck Oil, Gamma Ray, and the Visual Genius of Nick Dwyer

Beavertown's breakthrough came with two revolutionary flagship recipes: Neck Oil (an exquisitely sessionable, crisp pale ale) and Gamma Ray (an explosive American-style pale ale loaded with tropical hop aromas). Unlike traditional brown glass bottles, Plant made the bold, controversial decision to can Beavertown's beers in aluminum, preserving hop freshness from UV degradation.

To differentiate his cans on supermarket and pub shelves, Plant hired talented graphic illustrator Nick Dwyer. Dwyer designed surreal, pop-art illustrations featuring Day of the Dead skeletons, psychedelic spacemen, and retro sci-fi motifs. The vibrant cans became instant design classics, capturing the youth demographic and leading the UK craft beer revolution.

Scaling Beaverworld and the £100+ Million Heineken Deal

By 2014, demand had outstripped capacity, prompting a move to an industrial park in Tottenham. By 2018, Beavertown was producing tens of millions of pints annually. To fund the construction of "Beaverworld"—a state-of-the-art 129,000-square-foot brewing complex in Enfield capable of brewing 500,000 hectoliters annually—Logan struck a strategic investment partnership with Dutch brewing titan Heineken, selling a minority stake.

In September 2022, after guiding Beavertown into Tottenham Hotspur's world-class stadium as the first official in-stadium microbrewery in Premier League history, Logan Plant finalized the sale of the remaining equity to Heineken. The transaction valued Beavertown at well over £100 Million. Plant stepped down as CEO to assume an advisory and brand ambassador role, cementing one of the greatest entrepreneurial exits in British beverage history.

Family Life and Estimated Net Worth

Logan Plant resides in London with his wife Bridget and their two children. Despite achieving immense independent wealth, he remains approachable, frequently seen supporting grassroots live music venues, independent food ventures, and charitable environmental foundations.

His personal net worth is estimated between $40 Million and $50 Million (£35M - £40M), driven directly by his majority equity liquidation in Beavertown Brewery, ongoing commercial property holdings, and private business investments. Through sheer determination and creativity, Logan Romero Plant achieved what few children of rock legends ever manage: establishing a household brand that stands proudly on its own global reputation.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Who is Logan Romero Plant's father?

Logan Romero Plant is the son of Robert Plant, the legendary lead vocalist and lyricist of the rock band Led Zeppelin.

What company did Logan Plant found?

He founded Beavertown Brewery in 2011, starting with a 50-liter homebrew kit in a London barbecue joint and building it into one of Britain's most successful craft breweries.

What are Beavertown Brewery's most famous beers?

Its two most iconic flagship beers are Neck Oil (Session IPA) and Gamma Ray (American Pale Ale), both recognized globally for their colorful, skeleton-themed pop-art cans.

Did Logan Plant sell Beavertown Brewery?

Yes, Heineken acquired a minority stake in 2018 and purchased the remaining equity in September 2022 in a deal valuing the brewery at over £100 million.

What is Logan Romero Plant's estimated net worth?

His personal net worth is estimated between $40 Million and $50 Million (£35M - £40M), earned primarily through the successful sale of Beavertown Brewery.
