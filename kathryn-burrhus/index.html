Who Is Kathryn Burrhus? The Biography of Stone Cold Steve Austin's First Wife and Life Away from WWE

From North Texas college sweethearts to the grueling early days of professional wrestling at the Dallas Sportatorium.

Before 'Stone Cold' Steve Austin became the highest-grossing box office attraction in the history of World Wrestling Entertainment (WWE)—shattering merchandise records, headlining WrestleMania events, and leading the historic Attitude Era—he was simply Steven James Anderson, an unheralded college football player trying to earn a living in North Texas. By his side during those formative, penniless beginnings was Kathryn Burrhus, his college sweetheart and first wife.

While Austin's later marriages to British wrestling valet Jeanie Clarke (Lady Blossom) and WWE Hall of Fame personality Debra Marshall played out across national tabloids and sports entertainment storylines, Kathryn Burrhus represents the antithesis of the pro wrestling circus. After their brief marriage ended in 1992, she deliberately stepped out of the spotlight, building a quiet, successful career in Texas corporate administration.

College Days at North Texas State University

Kathryn Burrhus was born in the mid-1960s in suburban Texas. She enrolled at North Texas State University (now the University of North Texas) in Denton in the mid-1980s, studying business and healthcare management.

It was on the Denton campus that she met Steve Anderson. Austin had transferred from Wharton County Junior College on a football scholarship, playing linebacker and offensive lineman for the Mean Green football team. Burrhus and Anderson bonded quickly, becoming a fixture on campus through Austin's collegiate athletic career.

Supporting Austin's Wrestling Dream at the Dallas Sportatorium

When Austin fell just a few credits shy of earning his physical education degree in the late 1980s, he took a job driving a forklift at a freight terminal in Dallas, earning modest wages. Passionate about pro wrestling, he enrolled in Gentleman Chris Adams's wrestling school at the gritty Dallas Sportatorium, paying $45 per week for instruction.

The Gritty Realities of Regional Territory Wrestling in the USWA

Before World Wrestling Entertainment evolved into a multi-billion-dollar global media conglomerate, professional wrestling in the late 1980s and early 1990s was anchored by grueling regional territories. After training under Chris Adams at the Dallas Sportatorium, Steve Austin was assigned to the United States Wrestling Association (USWA), wrestling across cramped armories, high-school gymnasiums, and state fairs in Texas and Tennessee.

Paydays were notoriously meager, often ranging between $15 and $25 per night, with wrestlers responsible for their own gas, food, and motel bills. While Austin endured marathon drives alongside legends like Dutch Mantell and Jerry Lawler, Kathryn Burrhus maintained their home base in Denton, Texas. Working a dependable corporate administrative job, she paid the household bills, managed healthcare coverage, and provided Steve with emotional grounding during stretches when the wrestling business offered virtually no financial security.

Marriage in 1990 and the Move to WCW

On November 24, 1990, Kathryn Burrhus and Steve Austin married in a quiet civil ceremony in Texas. Shortly thereafter, Austin received his first major professional breakthrough: a contract with World Championship Wrestling (WCW) in Atlanta, Georgia, where he was packaged as "Stunning Steve Austin."

The transition to national television brought grueling travel demands. Austin spent over 250 days a year on the road across the southeastern wrestling circuit. The distance and the rapid escalation of Austin's career placed acute strain on the young marriage.

The Evolution of 'Stunning Steve' and the Road to Atlanta

Austin's big break arrived in 1991 when World Championship Wrestling (WCW) executive Jim Herd and head booker Dusty Rhodes signed him to a national contract. Packaged as 'Stunning Steve Austin,' a sleek, arrogant blond athlete in dazzling robes, Austin quickly captured the WCW World Television Championship by defeating Bobby Eaton in Birmingham, Alabama.

However, the move to WCW meant relocating to Atlanta and entering a grueling touring schedule exceeding 250 days per year. While Austin's star was ascending rapidly on national television broadcasts on TBS, the extreme physical demands and near-constant separation created an insurmountable divide between his road life and Kathryn's desire for a stable, quiet existence in Texas. Their 1992 annulment was the quiet, dignified culmination of two lives moving in fundamentally opposite directions.

1992 Annulment and Dignified Silence

While working in WCW, Austin began a romantic relationship with British wrestling valet Jeanie Clarke, known on television as Lady Blossom. Recognizing that their marriage could not withstand the reality of the wrestling business, Austin and Burrhus legally annulled their marriage on August 7, 1992, after less than two years of marriage.

Following the annulment, Burrhus made the conscious choice to sever all ties with the professional wrestling community. While other figures in Austin's orbit published memoirs or granted tell-all interviews to wrestling podcasts, Burrhus never monetized her past relationship, preserving her dignity and privacy completely.

Steve Austin's Reflections in 'The Stone Cold Truth'

In his 2003 bestselling autobiography, The Stone Cold Truth (co-authored with Jim Ross and Dennis Brent), Steve Austin reflected candidly on his early marriage to Kathryn Burrhus. Austin wrote with genuine gratitude and respect for Kathryn's sacrifices during his hungry years in Dallas and Memphis, openly acknowledging that his relentless ambition and single-minded obsession with wrestling had made it impossible for him to be a dependable husband at that stage of his life.

Austin commended Kathryn for her grace, maturity, and complete refusal to harbor public bitterness or seek financial exploitation after their annulment. That mutual respect remains a defining hallmark of their past relationship, setting Kathryn apart from the turbulent controversies that marked Austin's subsequent high-profile marriages.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How did Kathryn Burrhus meet Steve Austin?

They met in the mid-1980s as undergraduate students at North Texas State University (now University of North Texas) in Denton, where Austin was playing college football.

When were Kathryn Burrhus and Steve Austin married?

They were married on November 24, 1990 in Texas, shortly before Austin signed his first major professional wrestling contract with World Championship Wrestling (WCW).

Why did Kathryn Burrhus and Steve Austin separate?

Their marriage was annulled on August 7, 1992 due to the grueling travel demands of WCW and Austin's subsequent relationship with wrestling valet Jeanie Clarke (Lady Blossom).

Did Kathryn Burrhus and Steve Austin have children?

No, Kathryn Burrhus and Steve Austin did not have any children during their marriage.

What is Kathryn Burrhus doing today?

She lives a completely private life in Texas, working in healthcare administration and corporate management with an estimated net worth of $1 Million.
