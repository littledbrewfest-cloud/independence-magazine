Ann Belsky: Her Broadway Design Career, Marriage to Rick Moranis, and Enduring Legacy

An in-depth biographical profile of Broadway costume and makeup designer Ann Belsky: her acclaimed theatrical credits, marriage to Rick Moranis, family life, and lasting arts legacy.

By Sarah Jenkins
Chief Investigative Journalist & Senior Biographer
9 min read November 2, 2026

Ann Belsky was an acclaimed American Broadway costume and makeup designer, recognized for her artistry on landmark theatrical productions including Death of a Salesman and Singin' in the Rain, as well as her marriage to comedy icon Rick Moranis. Following her tragic death from breast cancer in 1991, her legacy endures through her two children and the Ann Belsky Moranis Arts Program.

During the 1980s, Ann worked behind the scenes on some of the most competitive stages in New York City. Her creative eye and technical mastery helped shape the visual identity of multiple award-winning Broadway plays and musicals.

Her family story and personal dedication parallel other artistic and private figures—such as Dalia Asafi preserving quiet domestic devotion while surrounded by public interest or Jillian Beth Gumbel maintaining professional authenticity away from entertainment sensationalism—highlighting a legacy defined by substance, love, and artistic integrity.

Who Was Ann Belsky?

Ann Belsky was an accomplished theatrical artist whose career spanned major stage productions in New York City throughout the 1980s. She possessed a refined aesthetic sensibility that made her a sought-after collaborator among directors and stage producers.

Her work required an in-depth understanding of character development, historical costume research, and rapid backstage execution. Stage actors and directors praised her ability to translate dramatic themes into compelling visual aesthetics.

Beyond her Broadway achievements, Ann was the devoted partner of actor Rick Moranis. Together, they cultivated a close, grounded family life that prioritized their children above all professional commitments.

Early Theatrical Ambitions and Broadway Design Career

From an early age, Ann demonstrated a passion for visual storytelling and textile design. She studied costume history and stage craft, immersing herself in the technical requirements of live theatrical performance.

She earned her breakthrough into Broadway through diligent backstage work in regional theaters and off-Broadway workshops. Her meticulous attention to lighting, texture, and character authenticity quickly captured the attention of leading Broadway producers.

According to the Internet Broadway Database (IBDB), the Broadway League's official archive, Ann was credited on multiple high-profile productions. Her contributions established her as a rising creative talent within the New York theater community.

Ann Belsky alongside husband Rick Moranis at a New York theatrical event during the height of their creative careers in the mid-1980s.

Major Broadway Credits: From Death of a Salesman to Singin' in the Rain

Ann's official Broadway portfolio includes four major productions that defined Broadway excellence in the 1980s. In 1983, she contributed to K2 at the Brooks Atkinson Theatre, a gripping drama set on a Himalayan mountain ledge that demanded extreme technical costume and makeup durability.

In 1984, she served as makeup designer for the landmark revival of Arthur Miller's Death of a Salesman at the Broadhurst Theatre. Starring Dustin Hoffman as Willy Loman and John Malkovich as Biff, the production won the Tony Award for Best Reproduction and was broadcast nationally on CBS.

Her versatility was demonstrated further in 1985 with Big River: The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn at the Eugene O'Neill Theatre. The musical won seven Tony Awards, including Best Musical, with Ann's design work capturing the rustic authenticity of nineteenth-century America.

Later that same year, she worked on the Broadway stage adaptation of Singin' in the Rain at the Gershwin Theatre, managing the intricate waterproof makeup requirements for live stage downpours night after night.

Marriage to Rick Moranis and Shared Creative Life

In the mid-1980s, Ann met Canadian comedian and actor Rick Moranis. Moranis had gained international stardom through SCTV and blockbuster films such as Ghostbusters and Little Shop of Horrors.

The couple married in 1986, establishing a home in New York. Despite Rick's surging box office profile with hits like Spaceballs, Honey, I Shrunk the Kids, and Parenthood, the couple maintained an unpretentious home environment centered on family warmth.

Colleagues remembered their marriage as a true partnership of mutual respect. Ann appreciated Rick's humble sense of humor, while Rick deeply admired Ann's artistic dedication and quiet strength.

Family Life and Raising Mitchell and Rachel Moranis

Ann and Rick welcomed two children into their family: their son Mitchell, born in 1988, and their daughter Rachel, born in 1990. Both parents were devoted to providing their young children with a normal, nurturing childhood away from public spectacle.

Both children grew up to inherit their parents' artistic sensibilities. Mitchell pursued writing, visual arts, and music, while Rachel earned degrees in architecture and design, co-founding successful creative ventures including Octave and Gateway Arts.

The deep values of kindness, education, and creative curiosity that Ann instilled in her children during their infancy remained a guiding force throughout their lives.

Illness, Untimely Passing in 1991, and Rick’s Hollywood Hiatus

In the late 1980s, Ann was diagnosed with breast cancer. She faced her medical treatments with immense courage and resilience, supported closely by Rick, her family, and a wide circle of theater colleagues.

Tragically, Ann passed away on February 15, 1991, at the young age of 34. Her death was a devastating blow to her husband, her young toddlers, and the broader theatrical community that respected her talent so deeply.

Following Ann's passing, Rick Moranis made the extraordinary decision to gradually step away from acting. Finding it impossible to travel to film sets while being the sole caregiver for two young children, he prioritized fatherhood over Hollywood stardom.

Moranis explained in later interviews that raising his and Ann's children in New York was the most important commitment of his life. His decision won widespread public admiration as an inspiring example of parental devotion.

The Ann Belsky Moranis Arts Program and Enduring Memorials

To ensure that Ann's creative spirit continued to enrich future generations, an arts education initiative was established in her honor: the Ann Belsky Moranis Arts Program at Ohr Torah Stone.

The program provides students with structured opportunities to study painting, sculpture, stage craft, and visual design. It honors Ann's belief that artistic expression empowers young minds and fosters emotional healing.

Generations of students have benefited from the workshops, exhibitions, and scholarships funded through the program, keeping Ann's creative vision alive across international borders.

Reflections on Her Creative Impact

Though her life was cut short, Ann Belsky's contributions to American theater and her family remain enduring. Her name is permanently etched into the history of Tony Award-winning Broadway revivals, while her legacy lives on through her accomplished children.

Her story is one of creative excellence, enduring love, and profound family sacrifice. In both the theatrical archives of New York and the hearts of those who loved her, Ann Belsky's memory remains bright and deeply respected.

Frequently Asked Questions About Ann Belsky

Who was Ann Belsky?

Ann Belsky was a prominent American Broadway costume and makeup designer, celebrated for her work on Tony Award-winning productions and her marriage to comedic actor Rick Moranis.

When did Ann Belsky pass away?

Ann passed away on February 15, 1991, at the age of 34, following a courageous battle with breast cancer.

What Broadway productions did Ann Belsky design?

Her notable Broadway design credits include Death of a Salesman (1984), Big River (1985), Singin' in the Rain (1985–1986), and K2 (1983).

Who are Ann Belsky and Rick Moranis’s children?

They had two children together: son Mitchell Moranis, born in 1988, and daughter Rachel Moranis, born in 1990.

Why did Rick Moranis leave Hollywood?

Following Ann's untimely death in 1991, Rick chose to step away from filmmaking to serve as a full-time, hands-on single father to their two young children in New York.

What is the Ann Belsky Moranis Arts Program?

It is an educational initiative established at Ohr Torah Stone in Israel that provides students with creative arts workshops, design training, and educational scholarships in her memory.
